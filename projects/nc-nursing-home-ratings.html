An exploration into NC nursing home ratings

September 20, 2026 · DTSC 2301, Portfolio Project 1

Notebook · Dataset (CMS)

Python, pandas, CMS API, linear regression

The question

How much does bed availability affect the quality of service and overall satisfaction with care that nursing home residents in NC receive?

I wanted to explore the bed-to-resident ratio of hospitals and nursing homes and see if the rating a facility receives is determined by that metric, and by how much. Intuitively, I would expect a dramatic decrease in rating for facilities that frequently have a shortage of beds, in turn increasing wait times and patient dissatisfaction.

That isn't what I found. The bed-to-resident ratio matters very little. Instead, overall_rating and staffing_rating are impacted much more by how safe the facility is by inspection, the number of times it has been fined, and the average amount of time per day that nursing staff can dedicate to each resident.

Starting with a disclaimer, parts of the code for this project were AI-assisted. I used it to fetch records from the CMS API and to work out certain pieces of syntax. I also want to acknowledge the limitations of the dataset. It contains general provider information about active nursing homes across the country, almost 15,000 records with 99 columns. To make the analysis more streamlined and precise I limited it to nursing facilities in North Carolina, reducing the record count from ~15,000 to 419. Analyzing that removed data could lead somewhere different, but that scope felt too broad to examine with confidence.

What the data showed

The first shows how nursing staff turnover correlates with the overall rating of the facility. As nursing staff turnover lowers, the rating raises. That suggests the importance of maintaining a satisfied nursing staff that is eager to hold their position long-term.

The second goes hand in hand with the first, describing another relationship between residents and nursing staff. There is a significant effect on how residents rate the staff based on the amount of hours per day that nursing staff can dedicate to each resident. If I were to speculate, a possible reason is that a nursing staff spread thin over the residents they are responsible for not only leaves the resident dissatisfied because of the perceived lack of care given to them, but the strain put on an overburdened nursing staff can also reflect poorly in behavior towards residents. This suggests further the importance of maintaining a satisfied nursing staff, with an emphasis on having an adequate number of staff who are able to dedicate more time in the day toward each resident.

The third shows the relation between the health and safety of the facility and the overall score given to it. The weighted health survey scores had the most dramatic impact on overall rating by far, requiring that highly rated facilities have a thorough and dedicated service and nursing staff to minimize the potential harm present towards residents.

The model

To finish off the exploration, I made a linear regression model with the variables I found to be most statistically significant and correlated with the target variable of overall rating.

Health survey score and nursing staff turnover have already been explained with the visualizations above. The third came out of exploring the correlation values between overall rating and the rest of the independent variables. The number of fines incurred by the facility had a significant relation, and I determined it would be a useful variable for the ethical reputation of a facility when rated by a resident, seeing as the two other variables covered health and safety, and nurse-resident relations.

Takeaway

The metric I expected to dominate, whether a facility has beds available, barely registers. What residents in North Carolina appear to respond to is whether the facility is safe by inspection, whether it has a record of fines, and whether the nursing staff have enough time in the day to care for them.
